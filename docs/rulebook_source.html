Tournament Format

This is a mixed-level doubles tournament. Every team pairs one Intermediate player with one Beginner player, drawn at random. The event runs in two phases:

Crown the champions

🎯 Phase 1 — Group Stage (Round Robin)

  • 24 teams are divided into 6 groups of 4.
  • Each team plays the other three teams in its group once (6 matches per group, 36 group matches in all).
  • A win earns 2 points; a loss earns 0.
  • The top 2 teams from every group advance automatically — 12 teams.

🃏 Wildcards — the final 4 spots

  • The third-place team from each group (six in total) competes for the last 4 knockout places.
  • These six teams are ranked by points; the best 4 advance as wildcards — making 16 teams.
  • If a points tie decides who takes the 4th wildcard, the tied teams play a tie-breaker match.

🏆 Phase 2 — Knockout & Seeding

  • 16 qualifiers enter a single-elimination bracket: Round of 16 → Quarterfinals → Semifinals → Final.
  • Seeding: group winners are ranked by points and drawn against the weakest wildcards first; remaining teams are seeded by points.
  • No two teams from the same group can meet in the Round of 16.
  • Lose once and you're out. Win and you advance until one team lifts the trophy.

🏓 How Scoring Works

  • Group and early knockout matches are a single game to 11, win by 2.
  • Semifinals and the Final are a single game to 15, win by 2.
  • Rally scoring is used for this tournament: a point is awarded on every rally, no matter who served. (Confirm with organizers if traditional serve-only scoring is preferred.)
  • Always call the score out loud before serving so both teams agree.

🟩 The Court

Playing area Kitchen (non-volley zone, 7 ft) Net
📏 A pickleball court is 20 ft × 44 ft (same as doubles badminton). The net is 36 inches high at the sidelines and 34 inches at the center.

Serving & The Kitchen

🤚 Serving Rules

  • Serve underhand — paddle contact below the waist, with an upward arc.
  • Serve diagonally (cross-court) into the opponent's service box.
  • Keep both feet behind the baseline at the moment of contact.
  • The serve must clear the kitchen and its line to be good.
  • One serve attempt per server. A serve that clips the net and lands in is played (no lets).

↩️ The Two-Bounce Rule

  • The receiving team must let the serve bounce before returning it.
  • The serving team must let that return bounce before hitting it.
  • After these two bounces, either team may volley (out of the air) — but only from outside the kitchen.

Why it matters

The two-bounce rule keeps the game fair and rallies longer by stopping teams from rushing the net to smash the return.

⛔ The Kitchen (Non-Volley Zone)

  • The 7-foot zone on each side of the net is the kitchen. You may not volley while standing in it or touching its line.
  • You may step into the kitchen to play a ball that has already bounced — but you must exit before volleying again.
  • If your momentum carries you (or anything you're wearing/holding) into the kitchen right after a volley, it's a fault.

Etiquette, Conduct & Day-of

🤝 Sportsmanship & Line Calls

  • Call your own lines honestly. When in doubt, the ball is IN — the benefit goes to your opponent.
  • If there's a genuine dispute with no agreement, replay the rally.
  • No coaching during a live rally. Keep a good pace — be ready to serve and receive.
  • Encourage your beginner partner. This is a mixed-level event built on teamwork.
  • Paddle-tap at the net after every match, win or lose.

✅ Player Checklist

  • Court shoes with good grip
  • Water bottle & a towel
  • Arrive early; know your group
  • Check the schedule between matches

🏓 Quick Rules Recap

  • Serve underhand & cross-court
  • Let it bounce twice to start
  • Stay out of the kitchen on volleys
  • 11 to win (15 in semis & final), win by 2

⚖️ Organizer Authority

  • Unsporting behavior may result in a warning, point penalty, or disqualification.
